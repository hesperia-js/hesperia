# Community Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want our community to be welcoming, safe, and equitable for everyone. Treat people with dignity and respect their rights and contributions, regardless of race, ethnicity, caste, color, age, physical characteristics, neurodiversity, disability, sex or gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, language, philosophy or religion, national or social origin, socio-economic position, level of education, or any other status.

Everyone who takes part in good faith and follows this Code has the same rights to participate.

## Behaviors we encourage

People bring different social norms, backgrounds, and native languages to this community. Our words and actions can land differently than we intend, so keep that in mind as you work with others.

You can be blunt about the code and still be decent to the person who wrote it. If you think an idea is terrible, explain why. The person proposing it still deserves respect.

Help make this community work well:

1. Respect the purpose of the community, its activities, and the ways people gather.
2. Be kind and honest with others.
3. Respect different viewpoints and experiences.
4. Take responsibility for your actions and contributions.
5. Give constructive feedback with care, and accept it in the same spirit.
6. Help repair harm when it happens.
7. Act in other ways that support the well-being of the community.

## Behaviors we restrict

Instances, threats, or promotion of these behaviors violate this Code:

1. **Harassment.** Ignoring clearly stated boundaries or continuing unnecessary personal attention after someone asks you to stop.
2. **Character attacks.** Making insulting, demeaning, or pejorative comments about a community member or group.
3. **Stereotyping or discrimination.** Characterizing someone's personality or behavior based on immutable identities or traits.
4. **Sexualization.** Behaving in a way generally considered inappropriately intimate for the context or purpose of the community.
5. **Violating confidentiality.** Sharing or acting on someone's personal or private information without their permission.
6. **Endangerment.** Causing, encouraging, or threatening violence or other harm toward any person or group.
7. Other conduct that threatens the well-being of the community.

### Other restrictions

1. **Misleading identity.** Impersonating someone for any reason, or pretending to be someone else to evade enforcement actions.
2. **Failing to credit sources.** Failing to properly credit the sources of content you contribute.
3. **Promotional materials.** Sharing marketing or other commercial content outside the community's norms.
4. **Irresponsible communication.** Irresponsibly presenting content that includes, links to, or describes restricted behaviors.

## Report a possible violation

Disagreements happen, even when people are trying their best to work together. A conflict by itself is not a Code violation. The behaviors in this Code give us a shared way to reduce conflict and harm.

For a possible violation tied to an existing issue, pull request, discussion, or comment, open that item's menu and choose **Report content**, then choose **Report to repository admins** if GitHub offers it. See GitHub's [guide to reporting abuse or spam](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).

Whether this option is available depends on GitHub and repository reporting permissions. Use it only for existing GitHub content; it is not a general private inbox for incidents that happened elsewhere. Do not post private details publicly or create a public issue, discussion, or pull request just to make a report.

Community Moderators take reports seriously and make every effort to respond in a timely manner. They investigate all reports. That can include reviewing messages, logs, and recordings, or speaking with witnesses and other participants.

Community Moderators decide what information about investigations and enforcement to share. At their discretion, they may discuss conduct and explain their decisions publicly, or keep some or all of a matter private. This does not permit sharing personal or private information without permission.

## Addressing and repairing harm

If an investigation by Community Moderators finds that someone violated this Code, they may use the following four steps to decide how best to repair the harm. They consider the incident's impact on the people involved and the community. They may skip lower steps when the violation is more serious.

1. **Warning**
   - **Event:** One incident or a series of incidents.
   - **Consequence:** A written warning from Community Moderators.
   - **Possible repair:** A written apology, taking responsibility, or asking what is expected.

2. **Temporarily limited activities**
   - **Event:** A repeated violation that already resulted in a warning, or a first, more serious violation.
   - **Consequence:** A written warning with a time-limited cooldown. The cooldown underscores the seriousness of the situation and gives the people involved time to process what happened. It may apply to specific communication channels or interactions with specific community members.
   - **Possible repair:** Apologize, reflect on your actions and their impact during the cooldown, and think carefully about returning to community spaces afterward.

3. **Temporary suspension**
   - **Event:** A pattern of repeated violations that Community Moderators have tried to address with warnings, or one serious violation.
   - **Consequence:** A written warning with conditions for returning from suspension. A suspension gives the person time to reflect on their behavior and possible corrective steps.
   - **Possible repair:** Respect the suspension, meet the return conditions, and think carefully about how to rejoin the community when it ends.

4. **Permanent ban**
   - **Event:** Repeated violations that earlier steps did not resolve, or a violation so serious that Community Moderators decide they cannot keep the community safe with that person as a member.
   - **Consequence:** Remove access to all community spaces, tools, and communication channels. In general, permanent bans should be rare, require strong reasons, and be used only when other remedies have failed to change the behavior.
   - **Repair:** No repair is possible at this level.

This ladder is guidance. It does not limit Community Managers' discretion to use their judgment in the community's best interests.

## Scope

This Code applies in all community spaces. It also applies when someone officially represents the community in public or elsewhere. That includes using an official email address, posting from an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.
